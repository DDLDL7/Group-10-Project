# National Electricity Grid Network Analysis — Final Report

## 1. Dataset description

The dataset is synthetic, generated by a seeded script (`generate_data.py`,
`random.seed(42)`), so every run is byte-for-byte reproducible. It is
grounded in Ghana's real grid operators (ECG, NEDCo, GRIDCo, VRA) and the
real West African Power Pool (WAPP), which interconnects Ghana with Côte
d'Ivoire, Togo, Benin, Burkina Faso, and Guinea. **All coordinates,
capacities, commissioning years, and connections are illustrative** — they
must not be presented as verified measurements of Ghana's actual
electricity infrastructure.

| Table | Rows | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `utilities.csv` | 10 | Utility ownership records |
| `substations.csv` | 44 | 36 substations across 10 Ghanaian regions + 8 cross-border interconnection/hub substations |
| `lines.csv` | 55 | Transmission/distribution lines, including intra-region mesh, inter-regional backbone, and WAPP cross-border interconnections |

## 2. Cleaning steps

`src/cleaning.py`'s `clean_and_validate()` coerces numeric columns, drops
exact-duplicate rows, validates that every substation's coordinates fall
within a West African bounding box, and validates that every foreign key
in `lines.csv` resolves. Running it on the generated dataset found **zero**
issues of any kind — expected, since the generator is deterministic and
internally consistent. `tests/test_cleaning.py` proves the checks
themselves work, using deliberately broken sample data.

## 3. EDA findings

- **Substations by region**: Greater Accra has the most (6), followed by
  Ashanti (5).
- **Voltage distribution**: 161 kV and 330 kV substations (the
  transmission tier) together make up nearly half the network.
- **Status**: 43 of 44 substations Active; 53 of 55 lines Active, 2 Under
  Maintenance.
- **Most-connected substations**: Mallam, Kumasi Central, and Cape Coast
  tie for the most direct line connections (5 each).
- **Utility footprint**: GRIDCo operates the most lines overall (24 of
  55), consistent with its role as the transmission-tier operator.

## 4. Network analysis (Task 2.1)

Modelled as an undirected graph (44 nodes, 55 edges; AC power can flow
either direction along a line). Key findings:

- **Centrality**: Cape Coast, Mallam, and Kumasi Central tie for highest
  degree centrality (0.116). Cape Coast also ranks highest by betweenness
  (0.525) — a genuine inter-regional bridge, not just a local hub.
  Bolgatanga Substation ranks highest by PageRank despite modest degree,
  earning importance from its connection to the Burkina Faso
  interconnection.
- **Connectivity**: the base graph is **not** fully connected — 3
  components (`[42, 1, 1]`). The two isolated substations are Savelugu
  (Northern region — left unconnected by the generator's random 55%
  intra-region linking) and the Conakry Transmission Hub (Guinea — never
  referenced in the generator's `border_links` list).
- **N-1 contingency**: removing Cape Coast Substation (the highest-degree
  node) **fragments** the network from 3 components to 5 (`[20, 18, 3, 1,
  1]`). Cape Coast is a genuine structural cut point in this generated
  network, not merely a locally busy hub — a more decisive resilience
  finding than the illustrative example in the assignment brief.
- **Structure**: 21 of 55 lines are bridges (single points of connection);
  the network splits into 10 communities under greedy modularity
  detection; global efficiency is 0.244, diameter of the largest component
  is 14.

This is a simplified, graph-based educational approximation — it does not
model electrical load, voltage stability, or protection behaviour, so
findings are presented as structural observations, not certified
engineering conclusions.

## 5. Geographic analysis (Task 2.2)

- **Distance verification**: independently recomputing line lengths via
  `geopy`'s geodesic formula shows recorded lengths run ~17% longer than
  straight-line distance on average — expected, since the generator
  deliberately multiplies straight-line distance by 1.05–1.3x to simulate
  real routing.
- **Distance categories**: 9 Short, 28 Medium, 18 Long lines; 13 of the 18
  Long lines are 330 kV, confirming the expected pattern that high-voltage
  lines skew toward long transmission runs.
- **Regional connectivity**: 39 intra-regional lines vs. 16 cross-regional
  — most of the network's connectivity stays within a region.
- **Substation clustering** (25 km proximity radius): 32 geographic
  clusters, mostly singletons with a few pairs and one 7-substation
  cluster — physical clustering is much sparser than electrical
  connectivity, since substations within the same electrical mesh are
  often tens of kilometres apart.

## 6. Business intelligence and reliability (Task 2.3)

All metrics in this section are explicit **proxies** — the dataset has no
real load, population, or outage-history data.

- **Utility footprint by voltage tier**: GRIDCo dominates the 330 kV
  (transmission) tier (9 of 17 lines at that voltage); NEDCo dominates
  11 kV (6 of 9); consistent with GRIDCo's transmission role and
  NEDCo/ECG's distribution role.
- **Maintenance rate**: Central region has the highest under-maintenance
  line rate (16.7%), followed by Greater Accra (9.1%); by utility, ECG's
  lines are under maintenance 10% of the time vs. GRIDCo's 4.2%.
- **Asset age profile**: 2000s-commissioned substations are the largest
  single cohort (11 substations); the two 1960s-era substations have only
  a 50% active rate, the lowest of any decade — a plausible fault-risk
  signal if this were real data.
- **Capacity concentration**: the top 10% of substations by capacity (4 of
  44) hold **25.1%** of total rated capacity — a moderate concentration
  risk indicator.
- **Growth opportunities (proxy)**: every cross-border single-substation
  region (Benin, Burkina Faso, Côte d'Ivoire, Guinea, Togo, and their
  border stations) is flagged, alongside Upper West — all have both
  below-median substation count and below-median total capacity.
- **Reliability risk proxy** (age + maintenance + betweenness centrality):
  **Cape Coast Substation ranks highest** — it is simultaneously the N-1
  cut point identified in Task 2.1, a moderately aged asset (34 years),
  and structurally important. This convergence across three independent
  analyses (network, geographic, BI) makes Cape Coast the single most
  consistently flagged substation in the whole analysis.

## 9. Limitations of using synthetic data

- All figures are generated by a seeded script for reproducibility across
  the team, not measured from any real utility, and must not be cited as
  facts about Ghana's actual grid.
- Graph centrality and the N-1 check are structural proxies — they do not
  model electrical load flow, voltage stability, or protection
  coordination.
- The business-intelligence metrics (capacity flags, growth opportunities,
  reliability risk) are proxies built entirely from synthetic
  capacity/region/year/status fields; a real assessment would need actual
  demand, population, and outage-history data this dataset doesn't
  contain.

## 10. Challenges faced and solutions applied

- **Merge conflicts with unresolved markers**: a teammate's merge commit
  left literal `<<<<<<<`/`>>>>>>>` conflict markers committed as real
  content in several files (breaking two notebooks' JSON and multiple
  Python modules). Resolved by comparing each side's actual committed
  content directly (`git show <ref>:<path>`) rather than trusting the
  conflict markers alone, confirming no unique teammate work was in the
  broken files before overwriting them with the clean, tested version.
- **Repeated stray root-level CSVs**: the generator was originally run
  from the repo root instead of `grid-analysis/`, duplicating
  `data/*.csv` at the top level — this recurred twice via merges and was
  fixed each time by restoring the canonical `data/` copies and removing
  the duplicates.
- **Scope management across a 3-week arc**: with Part A (weekly tasks),
  Part B (a second, tutorial-style walkthrough), and later Business
  Intelligence/visualization/dashboard tasks all technically in scope,
  logic was consolidated into one `src/` package (`cleaning.py`,
  `network.py`, `geo.py`, `bi.py`, `viz.py`) reused by every notebook, the
  dashboard, and the test suite — avoiding the same analysis being
  reimplemented three different, possibly inconsistent, ways.
